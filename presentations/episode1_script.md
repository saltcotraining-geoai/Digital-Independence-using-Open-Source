# Episode 1 — Your Old Laptop Is Not Dead
**Duration:** 20 minutes
**Target:** Absolute beginners, Sudanese communities, 2-4 GB RAM laptops
**Approach:** Zero terminal. Pure GUI showcase.
**Instructor:** Saltibin

---

## 0:00-1:30 — Hook

### Visual
Show your Linux desktop. Open the app menu. Scroll through the categories slowly.

### Script
"Salam aleikum. Welcome to Digital Independence using Open Source.

Look at this screen. This is Linux Mint — a completely free operating system running on a laptop with only 4 gigabytes of RAM. No Windows. No macOS. And it's running all these applications without slowing down.

I know what you're thinking: 'Is Linux hard? Do I need to memorize commands? Will I lose my files?'

The answer is no, no, and no.

In this episode, I'm going to show you what Linux can do — by simply opening applications and showing you. No terminal. No commands. Just clicking, like you already know how to do.

And at the end, I'll show you something special: what this looks like on an actual old laptop booting from a USB stick."

---

## 1:30-4:00 — Office & Creative (Your Comfort Zone)

### Visual
Open the menu. Click **Office** category. Launch **LibreOffice Writer**.

### Script
"Let's start with the category most of us use every day — Office.

Here in the menu, under Office, I have LibreOffice. This is a complete replacement for Microsoft Office. Writer is like Word. Calc is like Excel. Impress is like PowerPoint.

I'm going to open Writer. Look — it opens a blank document. I can type in Arabic or English. I can save, print, export to PDF. All for free.

Let me show you another one — GIMP. This is like Photoshop. I can edit photos, resize images, design graphics. And it runs perfectly on this old machine.

If you've ever paid for Microsoft Office or Adobe Photoshop, I want you to remember this moment. Because these free alternatives do the same job. And they come pre-installed or one click away."

---

## 4:00-6:00 — Engineering & Design Tools

### Visual
Open **Graphics** and **Science** categories in the menu. Point at icons.

### Script
"This next section is for our engineers, surveyors, and designers.

In the menu, you can see FreeCAD — this is a 3D modeling tool used by engineers. It's like SolidWorks or AutoCAD, but free.

Here is QGIS — this is for mapping and geographic data. Surveyors and geospatial analysts use this every day in their work. In future levels, we'll learn QGIS in depth.

And KiCad — for electronics engineers who design circuit boards.

Now, I have to be honest with you: I am not an expert in all of these tools. But I know they exist, they are free, and they work on old hardware.

If you are an engineer or designer, this is your invitation to explore. The tools are waiting for you."

---

## 6:00-8:30 — Education for All Levels

### Visual
Open **Education** category. Launch **GCompris**, then **Kiwix**.

### Script
"For our teachers and students, look at this.

Under Education, you'll find GCompris. This is a collection of educational games for young children — math, reading, science. If you have kids or teach primary school, this is a complete learning tool that works offline.

And this — Kiwix. This is one of the most powerful tools on Linux. Kiwix lets you download Wikipedia, Khan Academy, WikiHow, and thousands of educational resources — and read them completely offline.

No internet. No data costs. Just a USB stick with the world's knowledge.

For teachers: Moodle is also available — a complete Learning Management System to run your classes, give assignments, and track student progress. We'll cover Moodle in a future episode.

This alone — the ability to run a school offline — is why Linux matters for our communities."

---

## 8:30-11:00 — Programming & AI

### Visual
Open **Programming** category. Launch **VS Code**, type a simple Python line. Open **Ollama**.

### Script
"For our developers and students who want to learn programming:

The Programming category has VS Code — the most popular code editor in the world. Jupyter Notebook for data science and machine learning. Python is pre-installed.

But let me show you something amazing — Ollama.

I can run artificial intelligence on this old laptop. Completely offline. No internet needed. Watch this.

[Open Ollama, run a simple prompt]

This is AI running locally on a 4-gigabyte RAM machine. Think about what this means for students who cannot afford cloud services or fast internet.

You can learn Python, write code, experiment with AI — all on hardware you already own. No monthly fees. No subscriptions."

---

## 11:00-13:30 — Media Production

### Visual
Open **Sound & Video** category. Launch **OBS Studio**, show recording. Open **Shotcut**.

### Script
"Now for content creators and media producers:

OBS Studio — this is what I'm using to record this video right now. It's free, open source, and used by professional streamers worldwide.

Audacity — for recording and editing audio. Soundtracks, podcasts, voice-overs.

Shotcut — video editing. You can cut, merge, add effects, export in any format. All for free.

And Blender — for 3D animation and modeling. Students in Europe and America pay thousands of dollars for software like Maya and 3ds Max. Blender is completely free and more powerful than most paid alternatives.

The message is simple: you do not need expensive software to create professional content. You just need Linux and the willingness to learn."

---

## 13:30-15:00 — Installing Software (GUI Way)

### Visual
Open **Software Manager**. Search for an app. Click install. Show a `.deb` file double-click.

### Script
"How do you get all these applications? Two ways.

First: Software Manager. It's like an app store for free apps. Click the menu, open Software Manager, search for any application, and click Install. That's it.

Second: if you have no internet, you can download `.deb` files on another computer, put them on a USB stick, and double-click to install. I'll show you exactly how in a future episode.

No terminal. No commands. Just clicking."

---

## 15:00-17:30 — Live USB Experience

### Visual
Switch to the 4GB machine. Show the desktop. Open **System Monitor**. Then open multiple apps.

### Script
"Now, let me show you this on the machine that matters — an old laptop with 4 gigabytes of RAM.

I'm going to open System Monitor first. Look at this — Linux Mint is using less than 1 gigabyte of RAM just sitting here.

Watch what happens when I open LibreOffice Writer, Firefox, and GIMP at the same time.

[Open them]

Look at that. All running. No lag. This same laptop would be choking on Windows right now.

This is not a special machine. This is a standard laptop that someone was going to throw away. Linux gave it a second life.

And the best part? You can try this right now without installing anything. Just a USB stick and 10 minutes."

---
